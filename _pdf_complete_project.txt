1. Project summary, problem and scope
Campus Pantry is a scheduling, attendance and inventory system for a campus food charity. Students book a half-hour session,
receive a QR code by email after confirmation, and scan it at the FoodHub front desk. A valid scan records attendance and
completes the booking. Bookings estimate expected demand; attendance provides a better count of people served. The system
also records purchases, product batches, physical stock counts and staffing for the operating day.
The design is revised from the original inventory-only draft. The updated ERD explicitly stores people and role memberships,
student bookings, sessions, shifts, staff assignments, volunteer registrations, purchase records, products, stock batches and
stock counts. Personal attributes are stored in Person once and shared by the role subtypes.
1.1 Problem and value
A charity pantry needs to estimate how many people will attend, staff the service periods, track purchasing and understand how
much stock remains. Booking totals alone are not attendance, and attendance alone is not exact food consumption. Combining
confirmed bookings, QR-verified attendance, purchase/batch records and physical counts gives the organisation better evidence
for ordering suitable quantities and reducing avoidable food waste.

1.3 Scope boundary
Included: people and overlapping roles; Student degree level and domestic/international status; 12 half-hour sessions; one

check-in; attendance/no-show status; shift and volunteer rosters; purchase records; products; uniquely numbered stock batches;
and physical stock-count history. The first version does not implement payment processing, supplier account management,
individual food-item collection tracking, or a fully secured production deployment. Access restrictions are a planned requirement,
but the immediate priority is a functioning prototype.

2.1 Confirmed booking rules
• Each booking references exactly one Student and exactly one Session.
• A student can make only one booking per day unless an administrator explicitly overrides the rule.
• A session has a maximum of 50 active bookings. A cancellation removes the name from the active roster and frees the place.
• On confirmation, the student receives an emailed QR code linked to the booking and UniKey.
• A front-desk QR scan marks the booking attended and complete.
• A student may arrive at any time during the operating day; late arrivals are accommodated without penalty and attendance is
associated with the session booked.
• Unscanned bookings are marked ABSENT after the operating day ends. No penalty is imposed.
• Walk-ins are asked to register for an available session unless an administrator creates a special booking.
• Admin exceptions and duplicate-booking overrides should be identifiable in the record.
2.2 Identity and role rules
UniKey applies to every person, including staff, students and volunteers. Person stores UniKey, name, email and contact details.
Student, Volunteer and Staff are overlapping roles: a person can be a student and volunteer, or a student and casual staff
member. Student-specific attributes are degree level (Bachelor/Postgraduate) and student category (Domestic/International).
These attributes belong in Student, not Person.
2.3 Shift rules
Each shift lasts two hours and has exactly two staff assignments: one MAIN_SUPERVISOR and one ASSISTANT. Volunteers
register for shifts and may also be students. For this timetable, each shift covers four consecutive half-hour sessions. The
outgoing supervisor hands over active volunteer coverage, unresolved booking/front-desk issues, special bookings and stock
activity to the incoming supervisor at 12 pm and 2 pm. No additional handover period is added to the timetable.

3.3 Key modelling decisions
Person identity is separated from role membership to avoid duplicate contact details. Booking and attendance are separate
concepts represented by booking status and attendance timestamp, because confirmation does not prove arrival. Purchase
headers and lines allow one purchase to include multiple products. StockBatch represents a distinct received lot and receives a
stable StockNumber. StockCount preserves physical count history instead of overwriting a single quantity on StockBatch.

4. Daily workflow and supplied roster
4.1 Operating sequence
1. Prepare the day's shifts and twelve half-hour sessions.
2. Confirm that each shift has a main supervisor and an assistant staff member.
3. Register volunteers for the shift they will work.
4. A student chooses a session; the system checks the daily booking rule and session capacity.
5. On confirmation, generate and email a QR code linked to the booking and UniKey.
6. At the front desk, scan the QR code and mark the booking ATTENDED/COMPLETE.
7. At the end of the day, mark unscanned confirmed bookings ABSENT.
8. Record purchases, create uniquely numbered stock batches and save physical stock counts.
9. Review booking, attendance, purchase and inventory reports to plan future quantities and reduce food waste.

5. Purchasing and inventory workflow
5.1 Stock number convention
Every distinct received product batch receives a globally unique StockNumber in the format STK-000001, STK-000002 and so
on. It is a stable internal identifier and does not encode product name, expiry date or quantity. If one product arrives in two
distinct lots or expiry dates, create two StockBatch records and two StockNumbers.

Supplier's lot reference; optional if unavailable.

5.2 StockCount entity
StockCount records physical observations, not a continuously changing live quantity. Each row stores a batch, time, counted
quantity, count type (OPENING, CLOSING or SPOT_CHECK), the staff member who counted it and an optional shift. Zero is a
real count. Previous rows are retained so the team can investigate discrepancies.
5.3 Stock and consumption interpretation
For a batch in a completed shift, the original draft's estimated-dispensing equation remains useful when opening/closing counts and
aggregate receipts/waste are recorded:
estimated_dispensed = opening_qty + received_qty − waste_qty − closing_qty
This is inferred stock reduction, not a direct measure of food handed out. It can include unrecorded waste, counting errors or other
stock loss. QR attendance helps estimate the number of people served but does not identify which products or quantities each
person received. If exact product consumption is needed, add aggregate distribution movements by product/batch; do not infer
exact food consumed from attendance alone.
5.4 Inventory workflow
1. Record the purchase and its product lines.
2. Create one StockBatch per distinct received lot and assign the next StockNumber.
3. Record received quantity and expiry/lot information where available.
4. Record opening, closing and spot-check quantities in StockCount/shift stock counts.
5. Compare successive counts and explain discrepancies without overwriting old count records.
6. Use last known counts, attendance, expiry and observed stock movements to inform future ordering.

6.3 Normalisation reasoning
First Normal Form (1NF): fields contain atomic values. Session attendees are represented as separate Booking rows, not a
comma-separated list in Session. Second Normal Form (2NF): attributes depend on the whole key; role assignment and
shift-count attributes remain in their associative relations rather than being repeated in parent tables. Third Normal Form (3NF):
person contact details depend on UniKey and are stored once in Person; student classification depends on the Student role row;
product details depend on ProductID; batch/expiry details depend on StockNumber. The design reduces insert, update and
deletion anomalies and keeps historical stock counts separate from current catalogue data.
6.4 Integrity boundaries
Primary keys and foreign keys protect entity and reference integrity. CHECK constraints validate status values, allowed
degree/category values, quantities, times and count types. Triggers are used for cross-row rules that cannot be safely enforced
by a simple CHECK, including booking daily uniqueness, session capacity, and the requirement for one supervisor plus one
assistant at shift closure. The trigger implementation should be reviewed and tested on the target PostgreSQL version.

8.1 Status semantics
CONFIRMED means the booking exists and a QR code has been issued. ATTENDED means a valid QR scan was accepted
and the booking is complete. ABSENT means no check-in was recorded by the end of the operating day. CANCELLED means it
is excluded from the active roster and capacity count. A QR scan should be idempotent: scanning an already attended booking
must not create a second attendance event.
8.2 Privacy and security staging
The system stores identifiable student information, so access should ultimately be restricted by role. For the first working
prototype, focus on correct data model and workflows; then add authentication, authorisation, protected QR validation, audit
logging, email delivery safeguards and retention rules before production use. Do not expose a database-owner connection
directly to users.

CREATE TABLE person (
    unikey varchar(30) PRIMARY KEY,
    full_name varchar(120) NOT NULL,
    email varchar(254) NOT NULL,
    contact varchar(40)
);
CREATE TABLE student (
    unikey varchar(30) PRIMARY KEY REFERENCES person(unikey),
    degree_level varchar(20) CHECK (degree_level IN ('BACHELOR','POSTGRADUATE')),
    student_category varchar(20) CHECK (student_category IN ('DOMESTIC','INTERNATIONAL'))
);
CREATE TABLE volunteer (
    unikey varchar(30) PRIMARY KEY REFERENCES person(unikey),
    is_active boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT true
);
CREATE TABLE staff (
    unikey varchar(30) PRIMARY KEY REFERENCES person(unikey),
    staff_code varchar(30) NOT NULL UNIQUE,
    is_active boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT true
);
CREATE TABLE pantry_shift (
    shift_id integer GENERATED BY DEFAULT AS IDENTITY PRIMARY KEY,
    shift_date date NOT NULL,
    start_time time NOT NULL,
    end_time time NOT NULL,
    status varchar(10) NOT NULL DEFAULT 'PLANNED'
        CHECK (status IN ('PLANNED','OPEN','CLOSED')),
    CHECK (end_time > start_time),
    UNIQUE (shift_date, start_time)
);

FROM purchase_record pr
JOIN purchase_record_line prl ON prl.purchase_id = pr.purchase_id
JOIN product p ON p.product_id = prl.product_id
GROUP BY pr.purchase_date, p.product_code, p.product_name
ORDER BY pr.purchase_date, p.product_name;
-- Q10. Confirmed bookings, attendance and absences by session.
SELECT s.session_date, s.session_id, s.start_time,
       COUNT(b.booking_id) FILTER (WHERE b.status <> 'CANCELLED') AS active_bookings,
       COUNT(b.booking_id) FILTER (WHERE b.status = 'ATTENDED') AS attended,
       COUNT(b.booking_id) FILTER (WHERE b.status = 'ABSENT') AS absent,
       ROUND(100.0 * COUNT(b.booking_id) FILTER (WHERE b.status = 'ATTENDED')
             / NULLIF(COUNT(b.booking_id) FILTER (WHERE b.status <> 'CANCELLED'),0), 1)
             AS attendance_percent
FROM session s LEFT JOIN booking b ON b.session_id = s.session_id
GROUP BY s.session_id
ORDER BY s.session_date, s.start_time;
-- Q11. Batches approaching expiry within the next 30 days.
SELECT sb.stock_number, p.product_name, sb.batch_code, sb.expires_on,
       sb.received_qty, (sb.expires_on - CURRENT_DATE) AS days_to_expiry
FROM stock_batch sb JOIN product p ON p.product_id = sb.product_id
WHERE sb.expires_on BETWEEN CURRENT_DATE AND CURRENT_DATE + 30
ORDER BY sb.expires_on;
-- Q12. Sessions at or near capacity (80% or more).
SELECT session_date, session_id, start_time, capacity, active_bookings,
       ROUND(100.0 * active_bookings / NULLIF(capacity,0),1) AS capacity_percent,
       spaces_remaining
FROM v_booking_attendance
WHERE active_bookings >= CEIL(capacity * 0.8)
ORDER BY session_date, start_time;

14.2 Important design limitations to resolve
• The code treats session capacity as a hard limit of 50 even when an administrator uses the duplicate-booking override. This is
deliberate because the stated admin exception did not explicitly authorise exceeding capacity.
• Session bookings estimate demand, not food consumed. If the project needs exact consumption by product, add aggregate stock
distribution/movement records.
• The roster provisionally assigns the first listed staff member as main supervisor and Parth as assistant because the original roster
did not specify which staff member has each duty.
• Student degree level and domestic/international status are nullable in the demonstration seed because those values were not
supplied. Populate them when actual records are created.
• The sample QR values and emails are synthetic. Real QR tokens should be generated with a secure random source in the
application and must not expose UniKey directly.
• The SQL has been assembled as a revised implementation draft, not executed in this document-generation step. Run each script
in order and fix any environment-specific issues before relying on it.

Seed-data sequence alignment
The sample seed inserts explicit StockNumber values STK-000001 through STK-000008.
Because the identifiers are inserted explicitly, advance the sequence before generating
new StockNumbers from the default. Add this statement at the end of 02_seed_data.sql
(after the sample inserts; it is also safe after COMMIT):
SELECT setval('campus_pantry.stock_number_seq',
              GREATEST((SELECT COUNT(*) FROM campus_pantry.stock_batch), 1), true);
After the sample data, the next generated number will be STK-000009.
If sample batches are removed or renumbered, set the sequence to the highest numeric suffix
rather than relying on row count.
