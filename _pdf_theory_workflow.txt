1. Updated system overview
Campus Pantry supports the daily operation of a campus food charity. Students book one half-hour session per day, receive a
QR code by email after confirmation, and scan it at the FoodHub front desk. A successful scan marks the booking as attended
and complete. Bookings estimate planned demand; attendance gives a better count of people served, supporting food
purchasing and waste reduction.
The operating day has three two-hour shifts. Each shift has exactly two staff members: one main supervisor and one assistant,
who can help cover operations when volunteer numbers are low. A person can hold multiple roles, such as Student and
Volunteer or Student and casual Staff.
Earlier framing
Revised framing
Inventory-only; no student/recipient records.
Person, Student, Booking and Attendance are core parts of the model.
Shift stock counts and aggregate dispensing dominated.
Bookings, QR check-in, attendance, shift staffing, purchasing and stock
counts are linked.
Roles were treated as single or mutually exclusive.
Person specialisation is overlapping; one UniKey can be linked to multiple
role subtypes.
Batch stock was not assigned a clear stock identifier.
Each received product batch gets a unique StockNumber; physical quantities
are recorded in StockCount history.
1.1 Intended outcomes
The system should enforce one booking per student per day by default, cap each session at 50 bookings, record attendance
from QR scans, accommodate late arrivals without penalty, support admin exceptions, show staff/volunteer rosters, trace
purchased product batches and preserve dated physical stock counts.

3. Booking, QR check-in and attendance workflow
Stage
System behaviour
1. Select session
Student selects one of the day's half-hour sessions. Session booking capacity is 50.
2. Validate
Reject another booking by the same student on that date unless an administrator explicitly overrides the rule.
3. Confirm
Save the booking and email a QR code linked to the booking and the student's UniKey.
4. Front-desk scan
A valid QR scan marks the booking ATTENDED/COMPLETE.
5. Late arrival
Students may arrive any time during the operating day; attendance is recorded against the session they booked. No
late penalty.
6. Cancellation
Remove the name/booking from the active session roster and free the place.
7. No-show
If no check-in occurs by the end of the operating day, mark the booking ABSENT. No penalty is imposed.
8. Walk-in
Ask walk-ins to register for an available session unless an administrator creates a special booking.
9. Admin exception
An admin may override the daily duplicate rule or create a special booking. Record the override and admin
responsible.
3.1 Booking and attendance statuses
Status
Meaning
CONFIRMED
Valid booking; QR code issued; not yet checked in.
ATTENDED / COMPLETE
Front-desk QR scan succeeded; booking is complete.
ABSENT
No check-in by the end of the operating day.
CANCELLED / REMOVED
Removed from the active roster. If cancellation history is retained, it must not count against session
capacity.
Recommended QR design: use a booking-specific random token/reference linked to UniKey in the database rather than putting
readable UniKey text directly into the QR code. This is a design recommendation; access control and broader security hardening
can follow after the core workflow works.
Bookings represent expected demand, not attendance or food collected. Compare booking counts with QR-confirmed
attendance to understand demand and no-shows. Attendance alone does not establish the exact amount of food consumed.

5. Role model, supervision and handover
5.1 Multiple roles
Use one Person record for each UniKey. A person may simultaneously or over time be Student, Volunteer and/or Staff. For
example, a student who volunteers has one Person record linked to both Student and Volunteer; a student hired as casual staff
can also have a Staff role. Do not duplicate name, email, contact or UniKey in subtype tables.
Student-specific attributes belong in Student: degree_level (Bachelor/Postgraduate) and student_category
(Domestic/International).
5.2 Two staff per shift
Each shift must have exactly two staff assignments: MAIN_SUPERVISOR, accountable for the shift and decisions, and
ASSISTANT, who supports the supervisor and can help run service when volunteer coverage is low. The assistant is staff, not a
volunteer unless they separately hold that role.
5.3 Handover rule
At 12:00 pm and 2:00 pm, the outgoing supervisor gives the incoming supervisor a brief handover covering volunteer coverage,
unresolved front-desk or booking issues, admin/special bookings needing attention, and stock deliveries or counts in progress.
The incoming supervisor acknowledges it. No extra handover slot is added because none was provided; the next shift starts at its
scheduled time.

6. Purchasing, stock numbers and inventory counts
PurchaseRecord, Product, StockBatch and StockCount are separate concepts. A purchase record documents the purchasing
event, a batch represents one received lot of a product, and stock counts record physical quantities at specific times.
6.1 Stock number convention
Assign each received product batch a globally unique stock number in the format STK-000001, STK-000002, and so on. It is a
stable internal identifier and does not encode product name, expiry date or quantity.
Field
Example
Meaning

Unique identifier for one received product batch.

Catalogue product in this batch.

Supplier's lot code; optional if unavailable.

PR-2026-0048
Purchase that supplied this batch.

Date the batch was received.
ExpiryDate
2027-03-31
Expiry date where applicable.
If a purchase contains several products, use a purchase header plus product-level purchase lines, or separate product-level
records. Every distinct received batch gets its own StockNumber. The same product delivered in two separate lots/expiry dates
should have two StockBatch records and two stock numbers.
6.2 StockCount entity
Field
Purpose
StockCountID
Unique identifier for a count event.
StockNumber / StockBatchID
Identifies the batch counted.
counted_at
Date and time of physical count.
counted_quantity
Observed quantity; zero is a valid count.
count_type

Inventory-only discrepancy note; no student personal details.
6.3 Inventory workflow
1. Record the purchase and product received.
2. Create a StockBatch for each distinct received lot and assign the next StockNumber.
3. Save received quantity, lot and expiry details where available.
4. Record opening, closing and spot-check quantities as StockCount events.
5. Compare counts and investigate discrepancies without overwriting prior count history.
6. Use latest stock counts, attendance, observed distribution and expiry information to plan future purchases and reduce waste.
A count history is preferable to one editable quantity field: the latest count provides the current known quantity while previous
counts remain available for reconciliation. If exact consumption is required, add aggregate stock-movement records for receipts,
distribution, waste and adjustments.

8. Decisions and recommended build order
Design choice
Recommended treatment
Admin overrides
Provide an admin override flag/reason and record who performed it. Broader security can follow after the
core workflow works.
QR token
Use a booking-specific random token linked to the person/booking; avoid readable UniKey text in the QR
code.
Capacity overrides
The user authorised admin exceptions for special bookings and duplicate bookings, but did not explicitly
authorise exceeding 50 per session. Keep the 50-person limit unless a separate capacity override is
approved.
Attendance closure
At the end of the operating day, mark unscanned confirmed bookings ABSENT.
Inventory quantity
Use StockCount history for physical observations. Add stock movements if continuously calculated stock
balance or exact consumption is needed.
Purchase structure
Use PurchaseRecord plus PurchaseRecordLine if one purchase contains multiple products; link each
received line to its batch or batches.
Food consumed
Attendance is not exact consumption. Record aggregate quantities distributed/wasted or reconcile receipts,
stock counts and remaining quantities.
8.1 Suggested build order
1. Person and overlapping role tables.
2. Shift, two staff assignments and volunteer registrations.
3. Session timetable, one-booking-per-day validation and 50-person capacity.
4. Confirmation email and QR generation.
5. Front-desk scan and attendance status.
6. Purchase records, product batches and unique stock numbers.
7. Stock-count history and basic inventory reports.
8. Admin exceptions and access restrictions, then broader security hardening.
8.2 Revised project narrative
Campus Pantry is a scheduling, attendance and inventory system for a campus food charity. A shared Person record supports
overlapping Student, Volunteer and Staff roles. Students book one of twelve half-hour sessions per day, with up to 50 active
bookings per session, and receive a QR code by email. Front-desk scanning records attendance and completes the booking;
no-shows are marked absent without penalty, and late arrivals are accommodated. Three two-hour shifts each have a main
supervisor and an assistant, with volunteers registered to shifts. Purchases are linked to products and uniquely numbered stock
batches, while dated physical stock counts help estimate demand, plan purchasing and reduce food waste.
